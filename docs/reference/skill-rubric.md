# Swimmer Skills Rubric

Source: Jason (Axiom Aquatics), Oct 9, 2026. Sections 1 to 9 are Jason's content, transcribed as written and then updated in line with Jason's Phase 1 answers (`skills-rubric-answers-2026-10.md`) and the Oct 14 2026 decisions doc (`decisions-screens-skills-2026-10-14.md`). Sections 10 and 11 are implementation notes.

The Oct 14 decisions doc is the source of truth when any of the three docs disagree. Section 12 at the end summarizes the open points still waiting on Jason.

Every skill is rated on its own, 1 to 5, across nine categories, and each rating feeds a dependency map that shows what's limiting a swimmer and what to teach next.

## Rating scale

| Rating | Label        | Meaning                                                                                               |
| ------ | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| (none) | Not assessed | Not yet observed. Never defaults to 1.                                                                |
| 1      | Emerging     | Limited understanding or execution; needs significant guidance, demonstration and frequent correction |
| 2      | Developing   | Understands the fundamentals; executes inconsistently; needs regular feedback                         |
| 3      | Competent    | Executes independently and consistently in normal practice, with some inefficiencies                  |
| 4      | Advanced     | Efficient, repeatable technique at higher speeds and under fatigue; occasional refinement             |
| 5      | Mastery      | Refined across speeds, distances and competitive conditions, with minimal breakdown                   |

"No concept" (observed, swimmer can't do it at all) is a separate label below 1 and is not counted in averages. "Not legal" is a flag on skills with a legality rule (start components, turns, breakouts, stroke kicks, breaststroke breath timing); it counts as 0 in averages and shows as a red DQ marker on charts.

## What every rating records

| Field        | Values                                                                        | Why                                                                       |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rating       | Not assessed, No concept, 1 to 5, Not legal                                   | The technique grade                                                       |
| Context      | Drill, Practice swim, Race                                                    | Separates what a swimmer can do in control from what holds under pressure |
| Capacity     | Distance (yd) or reps held at that rating                                     | Keeps physical conditioning separate from technique                       |
| Measurement  | Kicks/min, cycles/min, stroke count, time, reaction (s), entry distance (ft) | Raw number for measured skills, so grades can be recalculated             |
| Fault tags   | Short list per skill (coach taps the ones that apply)                         | So two swimmers with the same grade show what's actually different        |
| Coach + date | Who rated it, when                                                            | Progress history and coach-consistency checks                             |
| Note         | Observation, correction, drill                                                | The coaching record                                                       |

**Technique vs. capacity:** the rating grades how well the skill is done; capacity records how long it holds. A swimmer with textbook fly who falls apart after 25 yd is a 4 or 5 technically, with a 25 yd capacity.

**Leniency rule:** grade against the expectation for the swimmer's age group (6&U, 7-8, 9-10, 11-12, 13-14, 15-18), then adjust up one band if their times sit in a higher motivational standard than their age suggests. A 5 for a 7-year-old and a 5 for a 13-year-old describe different absolute performances.

## 1. Body Position & Balance

The foundation category: almost every stroke skill builds on these.

| Skill                   | Applies to                                 | Type            | Builds on                           |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Buoyancy & balance      | Swimmer                                    | Rubric          | Head position (overall), Core stability |
| Head position (overall) | Swimmer; rollup of the 4 per-stroke grades | Rollup          | (calculated from per-stroke)        |
| Core stability          | Swimmer                                    | Rubric          | (none)                              |
| Static streamline       | Swimmer                                    | Rubric          | Core stability                      |
| Finger spacing          | Swimmer                                    | Rubric          | (none)                              |
| Sculling / feel for the water | Swimmer                              | Rubric          | (none)                              |

Horizontal alignment, hip position and body rotation are now rated per stroke and live under their stroke's section.

### Descriptors: Head position (applies per stroke, then rolls up)

1. **Emerging:** head lifts or swings to look forward or breathe; hips drop visibly.
2. **Developing:** neutral when cued; drifts up when swimming fast or breathing.
3. **Competent:** neutral spine, eyes in the correct line for the stroke, at practice pace.
4. **Advanced:** stays neutral at race pace and through breaths; minimal bounce.
5. **Mastery:** stays neutral through the full race distance, including breaths, turns and the finish.

## 2. Breathing & Coordination

| Skill                           | Applies to                         | Type                    | Builds on                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Exhalation                      | Swimmer                            | Rubric                  | (none)                                          |
| Breath control                  | Swimmer; graded per event distance | Rubric                  | Exhalation                                      |
| Breath timing                   | Free (L/R), breast, fly            | Rubric                  | Stroke's head position + stroke timing          |
| Stroke integrity on breath      | Free (L/R), breast, fly            | Rubric                  | Breath timing; freestyle also builds on Rotation |
| Rhythmic breathing              | Free, fly, back                    | Rubric                  | Exhalation, Head position                       |
| Bilateral breathing             | Free                               | Rollup (calculated)     | Lower of the L / R freestyle breath timing grades |

Backstroke has no breath timing or stroke integrity on breath — rhythmic breathing only. Breaststroke breath timing has a Not-legal option (the head must break the surface every stroke cycle). Breathing pattern (every stroke, every 2, every 3) is recorded on fly and free ratings; each swimmer has a coach-set recommended breathing pattern.

### Breath control (by event distance)

Graded against the event length being trained, so one swimmer can be a 5 at 25 yd and a 2 at 50 yd.

| Age group | 25 yd no breath     | 50 yd single breath                                    |
| --------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 5-7       | Mastery expectation | Not expected                                           |
| 8-9       | Mastery expectation | Mastery expectation                                    |
| 10+       | Assumed             | Expected; grade breathing pattern at race pace instead |

### Descriptors: Breath control

1. **Emerging:** breathes every 1-2 strokes; stops or stands to breathe.
2. **Developing:** reaches the flags before the first breath.
3. **Competent:** covers most of the distance on the target breaths; stroke shortens near the wall.
4. **Advanced:** hits the target breaths, but stroke or tempo falls apart at the finish.
5. **Mastery:** hits the target breaths at race tempo, repeatable on normal rest.

### Descriptors: Catch

1. **Emerging:** hand slaps or pushes down; elbow drops first; no feel for the water.
2. **Developing:** fingertips point down when cued; elbow still drops on most strokes.
3. **Competent:** early vertical forearm on most strokes at practice pace; elbow stays above the hand.
4. **Advanced:** consistent high-elbow catch at race pace and on the breathing side.
5. **Mastery:** high-elbow catch holds through the full race distance with no slipping at the finish.

### Descriptors: Swimming straight

1. **Emerging:** zig-zags or hits the lane line within a few strokes.
2. **Developing:** drifts but corrects; touches the lane line once or twice per length.
3. **Competent:** holds a line at practice pace; uses flags or ceiling to orient.
4. **Advanced:** stays straight at race pace; occasional drift on the last few strokes.
5. **Mastery:** stays centered at race pace through the full race, including into the wall.

| Hip position               | Rubric           |              | (none)                                |

| Finishing                  | Rubric           |              | Wall approach, Timing                 |

Breaststroke "Breathing" is removed — covered by breaststroke breath timing in section 2.

### Descriptors: Kick mechanics

1. **Emerging:** scissor or flutter kick; feet not turned out.
2. **Developing:** whip kick shape when cued; feet turn out on some kicks; knees too wide.
3. **Competent:** feet flexed and turned out on every kick at practice pace; knees about hip-width; legal.
4. **Advanced:** narrow, fast whip at race pace; legs finish together into streamline.
5. **Mastery:** powerful, legal kick holds through the full race distance with no shortening at the finish.

4. **Advanced:** tight, controlled undulation at race pace; hips stay near the surface.
5. **Mastery:** undulation drives the stroke through the full race distance, including breathing strokes.

### Underwaters and breakouts

| Skill                   | Applies to            | Type   | Builds on                                 |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Underwater dolphin kick | Free (own grade)      | Rubric | Static streamline, Fly dolphin kick       |
| Underwater dolphin kick | Fly (own grade)       | Rubric | Static streamline, Fly dolphin kick       |
| Underwater dolphin kick | Back (own grade; higher speed/distance standard) | Rubric | Static streamline, Fly dolphin kick |
| Pullout                 | Breast                | Rubric | Static streamline, Kick mechanics         |
| Push-off                | All                   | Rubric | Static streamline                         |
| Streamline              | All (one shared grade shown after every start and push-off) | Rubric | Static streamline |
| Breakout                | Free                  | Rubric | Underwater dolphin kick (free), Stroke timing |
| Breakout                | Back                  | Rubric | Underwater dolphin kick (back), Stroke timing |
| Breakout                | Breast                | Rubric | Pullout, Breaststroke timing              |
| Breakout                | Fly                   | Rubric | Underwater dolphin kick (fly), Two-kick rhythm |

Fault tag "breaks down 2nd length" is available on every underwaters, breakout and swim skill. Not legal applies to breakouts (surfacing past 15 m is Not legal; illegal breaststroke breakouts are Not legal).

### Descriptors: Underwater dolphin kick

1. **Emerging:** holds streamline only a few yards; arms separate or head lifts; kick small and irregular.
2. **Developing:** recognizable streamline to about the flags; kick present but slow and from the knees.
3. **Competent:** roughly half the pool with locked arms and head between them; moderate tempo.
4. **Advanced:** most or all of the pool with proper form and high kick tempo, but not repeatable lap after lap.
5. **Mastery:** full pool underwater with proper form and high kick tempo, repeatable lap after lap on adequate rest.

Same descriptors for all three strokes; the backstroke expected distance sits slightly ahead of free/fly.

### Starts

Track start, relay start and backstroke start are separate skills. Track and relay are rated on six components each; backstroke is rated on five. The start's rating is the average of the components, rounded down. If any component is Not legal, the whole start shows Not legal.

Track and relay components: Reaction (measured, s), Acceleration / force, Height, Distance (measured, ft), Head-arm-hand alignment on entry, Leg and feet position on entry.

Backstroke components: Reaction (measured, s), Push and drive, Arch and entry angle, Distance (measured, ft), Head-arm-hand alignment on entry.

The shared Streamline skill is shown after every start, so streamline off the start is graded once and flows through all three.

Open: Block setup / stance as a 7th component for track and relay starts (undecided).

### Turns

Legality first: a Not-legal flag applies to any turn; fault tags and the 1–5 grade still record.

| Turn                          | Used in     | What a 5 looks like                                                            |
| ----------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Wall approach                 | All         | Adjusts stroke without gliding or chopping; no breath into the wall (free)     |
| Freestyle flip turn           | Free        | Tight tuck; feet plant and push on the back or side into streamline            |
| Backstroke flip turn          | Back        | Legal single-arm pull onto the stomach, immediate flip, pushes off on the back |
| Breaststroke open turn        | Breast      | Legal two-hand touch, fast elbow-back turn, low push into pullout              |
| Butterfly open turn           | Fly         | Legal two-hand touch, same fast open turn into dolphin underwaters             |
| Fly to back (open turn)       | IM          | Two-hand touch, falls back onto the back off the wall                          |
| Back to breast (open turn)    | IM          | Touch on the back, immediate open turn into pullout                            |
| Back to breast (bucket turn)  | IM          | Legal reverse somersault from the back to the breast into pullout              |
| Back to breast (crossover turn) | IM        | One-shoulder crossover onto the front, immediate flip into pullout             |
| Breast to free (open turn)    | IM          | Two-hand touch, open turn into freestyle underwaters                           |

Tempo, stroke count and finishing are rated per stroke and live in their stroke's section.

### Descriptors: Pacing

1. **Emerging:** sprints the start and dies; second half far slower than the first.
2. **Developing:** aware of going out too fast; splits still widely uneven.
3. **Competent:** splits within a reasonable fade for age and event.
4. **Advanced:** even or slightly negative splits in most races.
5. **Mastery:** executes a planned split strategy and adjusts it in-race.

## 10. Dependency map and Jason's notes

When a skill rates low, the app walks down the "Builds on" chain. A foundation skill counts as low if it's 2 or below, or lower than the skill it supports. "Not legal" and "No concept" always count as low. The walk keeps going down the foundation chain while each skill is also low, and stops at the first one that isn't. The deepest low skill is the top coaching priority.

**Example (freestyle breath timing):** a swimmer rated 2 in freestyle breath timing with a 2 in freestyle rotation and 4s elsewhere gets freestyle rotation, and behind it core stability, as the coaching priority.

## 11. Implementation notes (catalog build)

These are build decisions, not part of Jason's rubric. Anything marked "check with Jason" should be listed in the PR summary.

**Skill splitting**

- Skills whose "Applies to" is each stroke become one skill per stroke (free, back, breast, fly): Breakout. Stroke-stroke skills with per-side grading (freestyle breath timing, freestyle stroke integrity on breath) carry a `rated_per_side` flag.
- Skills with a specific list of strokes stay one skill with the list stored in `applies_to`: Push-off (all), Streamline (all), Pullout (breast), IM stroke-change turns (back, breast, free, fly as appropriate).
- Underwater dolphin kick is three separate skills (free, fly, back) — same descriptors, different expected distance band for back.
- Breath control is one skill. Grading per event distance is evaluation logic, not separate skills.
- Head position (overall) is type `rollup`, calculated from the four per-stroke head position skills, never rated directly. Rollup = average of the rated strokes, rounded down.
- Track start and Relay start are type `composite`, each with the six start components as child skills (`parent_skill_id`). Backstroke start is composite with five components. Reaction (s) and Distance (ft) are measured. The start's grade is the average of components, rounded down; any Not-legal component makes the whole start Not legal.
- All turns are rubric skills in the Starts, Turns & Underwaters category.
- Golf score is type `score`; all four strokes have one. Breaststroke "Propulsion efficiency" was renamed to the breaststroke golf score (slug `breast-propulsion-efficiency` kept).
- Shared Streamline (slug `streamline-in-motion`, display name "Streamline") is one rubric skill with `applies_to = {free, back, breast, fly}`, shown after every start and every push-off.

**Descriptors**

- Loaded descriptor sets: Head position (per stroke), Breath control, Exhalation, Freestyle breath timing (L/R same wording), Freestyle stroke integrity on breath (L/R same wording), Breaststroke breath timing, Butterfly breath timing, Freestyle catch, Backstroke swimming straight, Breaststroke kick mechanics, Butterfly body undulation, Underwater dolphin kick (all three strokes), Pacing. Streamline descriptors are drafts pending Jason review.
- Skills without descriptors fall back to the generic rating scale meanings.

**Builds-on resolution**

- Ambiguous names resolve to the same stroke's skill first, otherwise the Body Position & Balance or Breathing & Coordination version. Examples: "Alignment" means that stroke's horizontal alignment; "Head stability" means backstroke's head position / stability; "Breakout builds on Stroke timing" means the same stroke's stroke timing.
- Breaststroke breakout and "Stroke timing" use the closest equivalent (`breast-timing`).

**Out of scope for the catalog**

- Expectations / leniency data (age groups, time standards), the breath-control age table, golf-score bands, and kick/stroke-rate target bands. These belong to later milestones once Jason answers the open decisions.
